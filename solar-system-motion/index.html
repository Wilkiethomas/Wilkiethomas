Four scales, one motion

How the Solar System moves through space

The Sun does not sit still while the planets circle it. It is falling around the Milky Way at about 230 km/s, bobbing through the galactic disc as it goes, inside a galaxy that is itself moving. Step outward through the four scales to see each motion added to the last. Drag to look around, scroll or pinch to zoom.

About the drawing. Distances and sizes are not to a single scale, since a Sun the size of a marble would put Neptune half a kilometre away. Orbit spacing is compressed at scale 1, planets and galaxies are enlarged everywhere, planet spin is slowed so it does not strobe, the Sun's vertical bobbing at scale 3 is exaggerated tenfold, and the galactic disc there rotates rigidly (in reality stars closer to the centre complete a lap sooner). Scale 2 offers a switch to the true proportions between orbital motion and forward motion. Planet surfaces are generated procedurally, not photographed.

Keyboard: Space plays or pauses, Left and Right change scale, R restarts, V cycles the view. Respects reduced-motion settings.
